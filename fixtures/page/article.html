For now, "most libraries in the U.S. do not own historical fiction," the AP reported. That's partly because historically-themed fiction has not been well received among librarians, who see it as a less scholarly way of telling a story and an inappropriate focus for an otherwise literary collection — one that is often described as a collection for children "with a history degree."

More importantly, though, historical fiction is often difficult for libraries to acquire in part because of the difficulty of finding original hardcovers in good condition. As the Los Angeles Times noted, "For more than 80 years, libraries have turned to hardcovers, not paper, for their acquisitions of rare books."
